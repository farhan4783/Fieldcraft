> **Challenge:** Hacktoberfest 2026 — Week 1, “Touch Grass”

## 1. Executive Summary

FIELDCRAFT is an audio-first, local-AI-powered outdoor mystery game. Before a walk, a player chooses a duration and starting area. The application retrieves nearby public points of interest (POIs) from OpenStreetMap, selects a small set of plausible checkpoints, and asks a locally running open-weight Gemma model to turn those real places into a short fictional mystery.

The player hears a clue, puts the phone away as much as the device/browser permits, walks toward the hinted public place, and receives the next story beat only when location checks confirm arrival within a checkpoint radius. The story is the experience; walking through the physical world is the game mechanic.

**Core idea:** The AI writes the mystery. Open map data grounds it in the world. Deterministic application code controls the rules. The player must move through the real world for the story to progress.

FIELDCRAFT is not a general-purpose chatbot, a photo-identification app, a turn-by-turn navigation app, or a generic scavenger hunt. Its differentiator is a **location-gated, audio-led narrative** with strict boundaries between AI-generated story content and code-enforced physical progress.

## 2. Product Thesis and Differentiation

### The problem

Many apps intended to encourage outdoor activity still require users to keep looking at the screen. Many AI outdoor projects generate a list of missions or ask users to photograph objects. These approaches can still make the screen the center of the experience.

### Our thesis

**What if the screen were only the doorway into a mystery—and the story could not advance until the player actually went somewhere?**

### The FIELDCRAFT mechanism

1. The player chooses a short walk and a tone, such as mystery, wonder, or playful detective story.
2. The application obtains real nearby public POIs from OpenStreetMap.
3. The application—not the model—selects and validates candidate checkpoints.
4. Gemma writes a fictional case and concise clues grounded only in supplied POI names and categories.
5. During the walk, audio and a minimal screen present one clue at a time.
6. Deterministic geofence logic unlocks the next story beat after the player reaches a checkpoint.
7. At the end, the application reveals the story and creates a short local field note.

### One-line pitch

**“FIELDCRAFT turns your neighborhood into a mystery that only unfolds as you walk through it.”**

### What sits above the AI

- **Narrative game design:** each walk has a premise, escalating clues, and a reveal.
- **Real-world grounding:** clues correspond to actual public POIs instead of imaginary locations.
- **Physical progression:** code gates story advancement using location checks.
- **Audio-first interaction:** the application encourages the player to look outward instead of continuously at the screen.
- **Local-first AI:** the mystery can be generated by an open-weight model running locally, without sending personal location data to a proprietary AI API.

## 3. Target User and Main Use Case

### Primary user

A student, city resident, solo walker, or pair of friends who wants a more interesting 15–30 minute walk without turning the walk into more screen time.

### Primary use case

> “I have about 20 minutes free. Give me a short, safe mystery around a public area near me. Tell me one clue at a time, and don't let the story continue just because I tap a button.”

### Product constraints

- The player must remain in control of when a walk starts, pauses, resumes, or ends.
- Do not encourage trespassing, entering buildings, approaching strangers, crossing roads unsafely, or going into isolated or restricted areas.
- The app must not claim that a place is safe, accessible, open, or currently available unless that fact is verified from a reliable source. The MVP will not attempt to verify these facts.
- Fictional story details must not be presented as real local history.
- A demo/simulator must be clearly labelled as simulated; it must never be represented as live GPS verification.

## 4. Scope: What We Will Build

The deadline is short, so the project is split into a **submission-critical MVP** and optional enhancements. The MVP is the product; stretch work must not delay a complete, tested submission.

### P0 — Required for the MVP

1. **Landing and setup screen**
   - Explain FIELDCRAFT in one sentence.
   - Choose duration: 10, 20, or 30 minutes.
   - Choose tone: mystery, wonder, or playful.
   - Request location permission only after the user chooses to create an adventure.
   - Offer a clearly labelled demo adventure if live location or map data is unavailable.

2. **Nearby POI discovery**
   - Use browser geolocation with explicit consent.
   - Fetch nearby public POIs through a backend endpoint using OpenStreetMap/Overpass.
   - Filter out unsuitable or ambiguous POI types and reject results with unusable coordinates.
   - Keep the number of candidate POIs bounded to reduce latency and avoid excessive external requests.
   - Display OpenStreetMap attribution wherever OSM data is shown.

3. **Checkpoint selection and validation**
   - Select up to three checkpoints for a short walk.
   - Prefer public parks/green spaces, public squares, public libraries, marked attractions, public artworks, fountains, and other clearly public mapped features.
   - Avoid selecting private residences, industrial facilities, highways, rail facilities, construction sites, or unmapped/ambiguous locations.
   - Use distance and a simple estimated-distance budget to keep the adventure proportionate to the chosen duration.
   - Do not claim to calculate an accessible or safe walking route. The MVP selects nearby checkpoints, not turn-by-turn paths.

4. **Local AI story generation**
   - Use Ollama with a configurable open-weight Gemma model, defaulting to `gemma3:4b` where the machine can run it; allow `gemma3:1b` as a lower-resource fallback.
   - Generate a short case title, premise, one opening narration, and one clue per checkpoint.
   - Ask for structured JSON and validate it in code before using it.
   - The model may invent the fictional mystery, but it must not invent factual claims about real buildings, local history, safety, access, or landmarks.
   - Provide a deterministic template fallback if the model is unavailable or returns invalid output.

5. **Audio-led walk mode**
   - Show only the current clue and very limited controls: replay audio, pause, resume, end walk, and reveal a hint after a delay.
   - Use the browser's Speech Synthesis API for narration where available; keep the text readable as a fallback.
   - Offer a low-distraction, dark screen with large text and no continuous map/dashboard during the walk.
   - Do not promise reliable background geolocation or guaranteed operation with the phone locked. Browser/device behavior varies; the MVP should be tested on the intended demo phone.

6. **Location-gated progression**
   - Use browser Geolocation API while the adventure is active.
   - Advance only when the player is within a configured radius of the active checkpoint and the reading's reported accuracy is acceptable.
   - Require more than one qualifying reading to reduce false unlocks from GPS jitter.
   - Do not allow a normal “Next” button to bypass a checkpoint in live mode.
   - Add a clearly labelled “Simulate arrival” action only in demo mode so the demo can be recorded reliably.

7. **Completion and field note**
   - Reveal the ending when the final checkpoint is reached.
   - Show elapsed time and number of checkpoints completed, with wording that distinguishes measured data from estimates.
   - Let the player add a short reflection or observation.
   - Save the finished adventure locally in the browser; no user account or hosted database is required for the MVP.

8. **Submission assets**
   - New GitHub repository created in the challenge period.
   - A working run-from-source README.
   - Clear architecture diagram and screenshots.
   - A short demo video, plus a hosted demo-mode preview if time permits.
   - A high-quality DEV article using the official challenge submission template/tag.

### P1 — Only after P0 works end to end

- Download/cache the generated story and POI metadata before the walk so the story can continue if the network becomes unavailable.
- A compact after-walk field journal with previous case titles and reflections.
- Replayable narration and simple vibration feedback where supported.
- More robust POI categorization and an optional map preview before starting.
- Model timing and failure telemetry that is local-only and contains no precise coordinates.

### P2 — Explicitly out of scope for this submission

- User accounts, public profiles, social feed, global leaderboard, or MongoDB.
- Real-time multiplayer mysteries.
- Turn-by-turn routing, road-graph optimization, or accessibility guarantees.
- Native Android/iOS apps or guaranteed background tracking.
- Photo-based checkpoint verification, image recognition, or training a custom model.
- Paid AI APIs as the required core path.
- Complex agent frameworks, multi-agent orchestration, or fine-tuning.
- Promises of precise safety, opening-hours, or live-access verification.

## 5. User Experience and Main Flows

### Flow A — Create an adventure

1. User opens FIELDCRAFT.
2. The landing page says: “A mystery is waiting outside. The story advances when you do.”
3. User selects duration and tone.
4. User taps **Create my mystery**.
5. Browser asks for location permission; the reason is explained before the prompt.
6. Backend receives coordinates for nearby POI lookup, filters results, and selects candidate checkpoints.
7. Local Gemma generates a story and checkpoint clues from the approved POI metadata.
8. The app validates the response and presents the case title, approximate duration, and a safety reminder.
9. User taps **Begin walk**. Audio begins only after this explicit user action.

### Flow B — Walk and unlock clues

1. App displays the current clue and offers audio replay.
2. The player puts the phone away as much as is practical and heads toward the described public place.
3. Geolocation readings are evaluated against the active checkpoint.
4. A single noisy GPS reading is not enough to unlock it.
5. On confirmed arrival, the app plays a subtle confirmation and reveals the next clue.
6. If the player is stuck, a hint can become available after a delay. The hint may reveal a more direct description, but it must not bypass the location gate.
7. The player can pause or end the adventure at any point.

### Flow C — Finish

1. The final checkpoint is reached.
2. The finale is narrated and displayed.
3. The app shows a summary and invites a short reflection.
4. The summary is saved in local browser storage.

### Flow D — Demo mode

1. User chooses **Try a demo**.
2. A seeded, clearly labelled fictional/sample area and route are loaded.
3. The user can progress with **Simulate arrival** buttons.
4. The interface labels this path “DEMO — simulated location; not GPS-verified.”
5. The README and demo video explicitly distinguish simulator behavior from live mode.

## 6. Technical Architecture

### Stack

| Layer | Technology | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Frontend | HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript | Responsive setup flow, low-distraction walk UI, browser geolocation, audio, local history |
| Map preview (optional P0 if quick) | Leaflet + OpenStreetMap tiles | Show candidate public POIs before the walk; no continuous map during the walk |
| Backend | Node.js + Express | Validate input, retrieve POIs, select checkpoints, call Ollama, validate AI output |
| Open-weight AI | Ollama + Gemma 3 | Generate fictional case premise and clues from approved POI metadata |
| Real-world data | OpenStreetMap via a suitable Overpass endpoint | Retrieve nearby mapped POIs; use reasonable request limits and caching |
| State | Browser memory + localStorage | Adventure progress and completed field notes; no account database |
| Testing | Node test runner or Vitest/Jest if already familiar | Unit tests for validation, geofence math, state transitions, and response parsing |
| Deployment/demo | Local runnable app + short video; optional static demo preview | Demonstrate real local AI path and provide a reliable fallback for judges |

### Architecture overview

```text
Browser
  ├─ Setup UI / geolocation consent
  ├─ Walk UI / Speech Synthesis / localStorage
  └─ Geofence evaluator (active-session coordinates only)
          │
          │ POST /api/adventures
          ▼
Node.js + Express backend
  ├─ Request validation
  ├─ Overpass client + timeout/cache
  ├─ POI filter and checkpoint selector
  ├─ Ollama client (local HTTP API)
  ├─ Story JSON validator
  └─ Deterministic fallback story generator
          │                       │
          ▼                       ▼
OpenStreetMap/Overpass          Ollama
(nearby POI lookup)             Gemma (local open-weight model)
```

### Key architectural rule

**Gemma writes narrative; deterministic code makes decisions.**

Gemma does not choose arbitrary GPS coordinates, determine whether the user has arrived, calculate route safety, validate geofence events, or write completion records. It receives a bounded list of already-selected POI metadata and produces narrative fields only.

### Local-first privacy model

- Run the model through the user's local Ollama instance.
- Do not send the user's coordinates or walking history to an AI provider.
- If live POI discovery is enabled, the backend sends a geographic query to the chosen Overpass service. The privacy notice must state this plainly; therefore, “no data ever leaves the device” would be an inaccurate claim.
- Do not save or log precise coordinates in application logs.
- Do not collect names, emails, or accounts.
- Keep saved stories and reflections in localStorage unless the user clears them.
- Provide a demo mode that uses fixture data and does not need location permission.

## 8. Data Contracts

### Adventure creation request

`POST /api/adventures`

```json
{
  "latitude": 28.61,
  "longitude": 77.20,
  "durationMinutes": 20,
  "tone": "mystery",
  "demoMode": false
}
```

Validation rules:

- Latitude must be numeric and in `[-90, 90]`.
- Longitude must be numeric and in `[-180, 180]`.
- Duration must be one of `10`, `20`, or `30`.
- Tone must be one of `mystery`, `wonder`, or `playful`.
- Enforce a request body size limit and request timeout.
- Do not accept model names, prompt strings, arbitrary URLs, or raw Overpass query text from the browser.

### Adventure response (illustrative)

```json
{
  "id": "local-generated-id",
  "title": "Case 001: The Missing Lantern",
  "premise": "A small fictional mystery begins in an ordinary public place...",
  "durationMinutes": 20,
  "mode": "live",
  "checkpoints": [
    {
      "id": "cp-1",
      "poiId": "osm-type-id",
      "displayCategory": "public green space",
      "latitude": 28.61,
      "longitude": 77.20,
      "arrivalRadiusMeters": 90,
      "clue": "A fictional, category-grounded clue that does not invent local history.",
      "hint": "A clearer clue that still requires travel to the checkpoint.",
      "arrivalLine": "The first part of the case clicks into place."
    }
  ],
  "storySource": "gemma",
  "createdAt": "ISO-8601 timestamp"
}
```

The values above are examples only. Do not hardcode these coordinates or names as real locations.

### Story generation JSON schema (conceptual)

```json
{
  "title": "string, 4–70 characters",
  "premise": "string, maximum 500 characters",
  "openingNarration": "string, maximum 280 characters",
  "checkpoints": [
    {
      "checkpointId": "must match a supplied ID",
      "clue": "string, maximum 280 characters",
      "hint": "string, maximum 180 characters",
      "arrivalLine": "string, maximum 180 characters"
    }
  ],
  "finale": "string, maximum 500 characters"
}
```

Validation requirements:

- The response must parse as JSON and conform to all length limits.
- Every checkpoint ID must match the approved input list exactly.
- Reject duplicate, missing, unknown, or reordered IDs if order matters to the game plan.
- Strip unsupported fields rather than trusting them.
- Escape output when rendering into HTML; use `textContent` for model-generated text.
- On validation failure, retry once with a repair prompt or immediately use the fallback story to protect the demo's reliability.

## 9. API Endpoints

### `GET /api/health`

Returns service health and whether Ollama is reachable. Do not expose internal environment values.

Example:

```json
{
  "ok": true,
  "ollamaAvailable": true,
  "modelConfigured": "gemma3:4b"
}
```

### `GET /api/pois?lat=...&lon=...&radius=...`

- Validates coordinate ranges and clamps radius to a server-defined maximum.
- Queries a suitable Overpass endpoint using a fixed, server-authored query template.
- Uses a timeout and small in-memory cache keyed by a coarse area cell, not exact user identity.
- Filters and normalizes OSM results before returning them.
- Returns a friendly error and offers demo mode if data is unavailable.

### `POST /api/adventures`

- Validates user preferences and coordinates.
- Retrieves/loads nearby POIs.
- Selects and validates three or fewer checkpoints.
- Calls Ollama with a bounded prompt and POI list.
- Validates model JSON and joins story fields to server-selected checkpoint objects.
- Falls back to a template narrative if model inference is unavailable.

### `GET /api/health` and optional `GET /api/model-status`

Only expose status useful to the demo; never return arbitrary Ollama logs, internal host details, or environment secrets.

For the deadline MVP, do not create endpoints for users, authentication, cloud storage, public profiles, or leaderboards.

## 10. OpenStreetMap / POI Strategy

### POI discovery

Use a fixed Overpass query template for a bounded radius and a limited group of useful tags. Normalize nodes/ways/relations into this internal format:

```json
{
  "id": "node/12345",
  "name": "Mapped place name, if available",
  "category": "park",
  "latitude": 0,
  "longitude": 0,
  "tags": { "leisure": "park" }
}
```

Some map objects will not have a name or a usable center coordinate; exclude them if the clue cannot be grounded reliably. Do not manufacture POI names. If the live query returns too few candidates, ask the user to expand the radius or switch to demo mode rather than inventing local places.

### Candidate POI policy

- Include only a small whitelist of appropriate public-place categories.
- Exclude residential/private-use features and infrastructure that could put a player in danger.
- Deduplicate near-identical points.
- Require coordinate validity and sensible distance from the start.
- Prefer a variety of categories to make each checkpoint distinct.
- Limit the adventure to at most three checkpoints for the first version.

### Distance strategy

For MVP, use the Haversine formula for straight-line distance and a conservative multiplier only for a clearly labelled rough walking-distance estimate. Do not call this a route calculation and do not present straight-line map connections as pedestrian paths. Avoid promising that selected POIs are accessible or open.

### OSM attribution and service use

Display “© OpenStreetMap contributors” with map/data attribution and link to the OpenStreetMap copyright page. Keep queries bounded, cache results briefly, and use a suitable Overpass endpoint. Do not rely on the core OSM editing API for read-only POI discovery. Hosted map tiles and Overpass endpoints are shared services, may be rate-limited, and are not guaranteed to be available; demo mode is therefore required.

## 11. Local Gemma / Ollama Integration

### Setup

1. Install Ollama from its official website.
2. Pull a Gemma model suitable for the development machine, for example `gemma3:4b`; use `gemma3:1b` where memory or speed is constrained.
3. Configure `OLLAMA_BASE_URL` and `OLLAMA_MODEL` in `.env`.
4. Confirm the model is available before requesting an adventure.
5. Call the local Ollama chat API from the backend, not directly from browser JavaScript.

### Prompting strategy

The backend supplies:

- the selected tone and duration;
- the case premise to create;
- a compact set of approved checkpoint IDs, names, categories, and broad distance ordering;
- explicit instructions that the story is fiction and local factual claims are prohibited;
- the exact required JSON format and length limits.

The model must not be asked to decide which coordinates are safe, to invent destinations, to infer history from a place name, or to decide whether a player has reached a checkpoint. It creates the narrative language only.

### Prompt guardrails

The system prompt should require the model to:

- produce a self-contained, family-friendly, concise mystery;
- write a distinct clue for each supplied checkpoint in the supplied order;
- use category metaphors when appropriate, but not fabricate real-world attributes;
- never ask the player to enter a building, private property, road, construction site, or restricted area;
- never ask a player to approach or photograph strangers;
- never reveal or generate new coordinates or POI IDs;
- return valid JSON with no Markdown fences or extra commentary.

### Reliability strategy

- Set a request timeout appropriate to local inference.
- Avoid generating long multi-page stories; target short outputs that fit the walk.
- Validate JSON with code.
- Retry at most once if the output is malformed.
- If Ollama is unavailable, use a deterministic template narrative based on the same selected POIs and clearly report that fallback mode was used internally; the user still gets an adventure.
- Do not require vision models or photo uploads for this project.

## 12. Deterministic Geofence and Game-State Logic

### States

```text
setup
  └─ create adventure → ready
ready
  └─ begin → walking
walking
  ├─ pause → paused
  ├─ end → ended
  └─ confirmed arrival → checkpoint-complete
checkpoint-complete
  ├─ next checkpoint exists → walking
  └─ final checkpoint → completed
paused
  ├─ resume → walking
  └─ end → ended
```

### Arrival rule

The client calculates distance from the latest valid GPS reading to the active checkpoint. A checkpoint can unlock only when:

1. The reading's accuracy is within the configured maximum (initial target: 60 metres; adjust after real-device testing).
2. The distance is within the checkpoint radius (initial target: 90 metres).
3. At least two qualifying readings are received over a short interval.
4. The current state is `walking`, and this checkpoint is the active one.

Use conservative defaults and test with real devices. Urban GPS accuracy can be poor; avoid tight radii that produce frustrating false negatives. Show “GPS signal is imprecise” with a retry/wait action instead of falsely marking arrival. Do not allow a client-side UI button to bypass the live checkpoint rules.

### Demo mode

Demo mode uses fixture checkpoints and an explicit simulated-arrival action. Every screen in that mode must visibly indicate “SIMULATED DEMO.” Do not store simulated arrivals as real location evidence or report simulated distances as measured walking.

### Safety and accessibility details

- Ask the user to begin only when they are in a suitable public area.
- Provide persistent pause/end controls.
- Never encourage phone use while crossing roads or moving through traffic.
- Display a brief reminder to stop in a safe place before checking the screen.
- Allow the adventure to be ended without penalty.
- Do not turn off or obstruct access to emergency calling or other normal device functions.
- Keep clues short and avoid audio that requires the user to read the screen while walking.

## 13. Frontend Design Direction

### Visual identity

- Name: **FIELDCRAFT**
- Tone: analog field notebook meets a restrained mystery interface.
- Palette: deep forest/charcoal background, warm paper/off-white text, muted moss accent, a small amber highlight for active clues.
- Typography: readable sans-serif for UI and optional serif accent for case titles.
- Motion: restrained transitions for clue reveal and case completion; no distracting continuous animations.
- Visual metaphor: case file before the walk, a nearly screenless field mode during the walk, and a journal/reveal after the walk.

### Essential screens

1. Landing page: concise pitch and one primary action.
2. Setup page: duration, tone, and location permission context.
3. Generating state: clear progress and a fallback message if local inference is taking time.
4. Case briefing: title, fictional premise, duration estimate, safety reminder, begin button.
5. Field mode: current audio/text clue, progress indicator, pause/end, optional delayed hint.
6. Arrival state: short success state, then next clue.
7. Finale/journal: story reveal, visit summary, reflection input, save button.
8. Demo mode: same flow with persistent visual “simulated” disclosure.

### Responsive and accessible requirements

- Mobile-first layout and large touch targets.
- Keyboard focus and visible focus indicators.
- Sufficient contrast and text scaling.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Audio text always available for users who cannot or do not want to use speech playback.
- No audio starts before a clear user gesture.
- Permission-denied and no-POI states must have an actionable recovery path.

## 14. Error Handling and Fallback Matrix

| Failure | Expected behavior |
|---|---|
| Location permission denied | Explain why location is needed and offer demo mode; do not repeatedly prompt |
| Browser does not support geolocation | Offer demo mode and mark live mode unavailable |
| GPS accuracy is poor | Keep current checkpoint locked, explain the issue, and allow retry/pause/end |
| Overpass timeout/rate limit | Return a friendly error; try a recent cache if available; otherwise offer demo mode |
| Too few valid POIs | Offer a broader radius or demo mode; never fabricate locations |
| Ollama unavailable | Continue with a clearly bounded template-generated story or show a helpful setup message; demo must remain usable |
| Model returns invalid JSON | Retry once with a repair prompt; then use template fallback |
| Speech Synthesis unavailable | Show the clue text and allow the player to continue visually |
| Browser tab suspends | On resume, reacquire location and validate current state; do not assume continuous location data was collected |
| localStorage is unavailable/full | Keep the active session in memory and warn that history may not persist |
| User exits mid-walk | Allow immediate exit; save only a partial summary if useful and clearly mark it incomplete |

## 15. Testing Plan

### Unit tests

- Haversine distance calculations: same point, known short distance, and invalid inputs.
- Coordinate and duration validation.
- POI filtering: allowed categories accepted, private/unsafe categories rejected, invalid coordinates rejected.
- Checkpoint selection: no duplicates, valid count, sensible distance ordering, no unsupported destinations.
- Story validator: good JSON accepted; malformed JSON, invalid IDs, missing clues, extra fields, and overlong strings rejected.
- Game-state machine: cannot advance out of order; cannot unlock in `paused`, `ended`, or `completed` state; simulated arrival cannot affect live sessions.
- Geofence behavior: one noisy reading does not unlock; poor accuracy does not unlock; two valid readings near target do.
- Safe rendering: model strings are inserted as text, not executed as HTML.

### Integration tests

- `/api/health` responds when Ollama is unavailable.
- Adventure endpoint returns a valid story from a mocked Ollama response.
- Malformed model response falls back correctly.
- Overpass failure triggers the documented path.
- Demo fixtures produce a complete adventure without location or network access.

### Manual end-to-end checks

- Complete an adventure using demo mode.
- Complete one short real-world test in a public, familiar area with a teammate.
- Check mobile viewport, denied location, slow model inference, audio disabled, and refresh behavior.
- Verify the location permission copy, OSM attribution, safety reminder, and demo labels.
- Record what actually worked, what failed, and any limitations for the DEV article.

### Definition of done

- [ ] `npm install` and documented startup commands work on a fresh checkout.
- [ ] Health endpoint responds.
- [ ] Live POI discovery can return usable candidates or a graceful error.
- [ ] Local Gemma generation works on at least one development machine, or the fallback path is clearly demonstrated when hardware prevents local inference.
- [ ] The story schema is validated before display.
- [ ] Live mode progresses only through deterministic geofence validation.
- [ ] Demo mode completes reliably and is clearly marked as simulated.
- [ ] No raw precise coordinates are written to logs or stored in hosted services.
- [ ] Basic tests pass.
- [ ] README, license, credits, screenshots, and demo assets are ready.
- [ ] DEV post uses the official challenge template/tag and is submitted before the deadline.

## 17. Demo Video Plan (60–90 seconds)

The video should prove the concept quickly rather than tour every line of code.

1. **0–8 seconds — Problem:** “Most apps ask you to look at your phone. FIELDCRAFT gives you a reason to put it away.”
2. **8–18 seconds — Setup:** choose a 20-minute mystery and create a case.
3. **18–30 seconds — AI proof:** show that a locally running Gemma model generates the case from real POI metadata. Briefly show the terminal/Ollama status or a small “Local Gemma” indicator.
4. **30–45 seconds — Field mode:** show the clue, tap play, put the phone away, and show a real outdoor shot.
5. **45–60 seconds — Physical gate:** demonstrate a real arrival unlock where possible. If using the simulator for any portion, show the “SIMULATED DEMO” label visibly.
6. **60–75 seconds — Reveal:** show the final story reveal and field note.
7. **75–90 seconds — Why open AI:** explain that the narrative model runs locally and the application rules remain auditable, replaceable, and deterministic.

Do not fabricate footage, results, walking duration, GPS proof, or performance statistics. If real device testing is limited, say so directly in the article.

## 19. Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Scope grows beyond deadline | High / high | Freeze P0 scope; no auth, database, multiplayer, vision, or custom routing |
| Local inference too slow or unavailable | Medium / high | Try `gemma3:1b`, limit output length, cache/fallback narrative, record actual model used |
| Model invents facts about POIs | Medium / high | Restrict prompt input, use schema validation, treat fiction as fiction, do not claim factual history |
| GPS inaccuracies prevent progress | High / high | Validate accuracy, use practical radius and consecutive readings, test outdoors, provide pause/retry and demo fallback |
| Overpass endpoint is slow or rate-limited | Medium / medium | Bounded query, timeout, small cache, no rapid retries, demo fixtures |
| Browser suspends tracking/audio | Medium / high | Test on target device, reacquire state on resume, don't promise native background behavior |
| UI looks generic | Medium / medium | Keep a strong analog case-file aesthetic and screen-minimal field mode; focus on typography and clarity rather than complex animation |
| Submission article is rushed | High / high | Draft the article outline while building; capture screenshots during real tests; reserve several hours for submission |
| Privacy claim overreaches | Medium / high | Disclose that live POI lookup sends a geographic query to an Overpass service; keep AI inference local and avoid coordinate logs |
| Attribution omitted | Low / medium | Include OpenStreetMap attribution and dependency/data credits in UI and README |

## 20. Final Product Rules

These are non-negotiable for the first release:

1. **The AI is the game master, not the authority on geography or safety.**
2. **The backend chooses real checkpoints; the model cannot invent or replace them.**
3. **The model writes narrative only; deterministic code owns location validation and state transitions.**
4. **Live and simulated progress must never be confused.**
5. **No model-generated HTML is executed.**
6. **No precise location history is uploaded to our own hosted backend or an AI provider.**
7. **The user can pause or leave at any time.**
8. **A finished, testable small project beats an ambitious incomplete one.**
9. **We document real limitations and test results honestly.**
10. **The story should make the player curious about the world, not keep them staring at the phone.**

## Decision Summary

Build **FIELDCRAFT** as a local-first, audio-led, location-gated mystery experience. Keep the MVP to three or fewer public checkpoints, short Gemma-generated narrative, deterministic geofence logic, a no-permission demo mode, and a clear local-first privacy explanation. Prioritize a complete end-to-end experience, actual test evidence, an excellent demo, and the DEV write-up over extra features.
